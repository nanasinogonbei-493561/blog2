<p><strong>プログラムやアプリは、必要な働きや安全を守れる範囲で、シンプルにするのがよいと考えます。</strong>余計な複雑さを減らすと、作る人は仕組みを追いやすく、使う人は目的の操作を見つけやすくなるからです。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ここでいう「プログラム」は、コンピューターを動かすための指示です。「プロダクト」は、人に使ってもらうために作ったものです。この記事では、スマートフォンやパソコンで使うアプリを例にします。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>もとになったのは、<a href="https://zenn.dev/nanashinogonbei/articles/should-programs-and-products-be-simple" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">Zennの記事「プログラム・プロダクトは、出来ればシンプルであるべきか？」</a>です。筆箱や借りた本を例に、作る側と使う側のシンプルさを説明します。以下のアプリや場面は説明用の架空の例です。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"ja: プログラムやアプリのシンプルさは、数だけでは決まりません"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">プログラムやアプリのシンプルさは、数だけでは決まりません</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":95,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/01-pencil-case.png" alt="必要な文房具が取り出しやすい筆箱と、物を詰め込みすぎた筆箱を比べるイラスト" class="wp-image-95"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>筆箱を思い浮かべてください。えんぴつや消しゴムに交じって、小さなおもちゃや使わない紙がたくさん入っていると、欲しい物を探しにくくなります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>使わない物を出し、必要な文房具を取り出しやすく並べる。これなら、勉強に使いやすい筆箱になります。でも、中身を全部出して空っぽにしたら、字を書けません。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>シンプルにするとは、目的を果たすために必要なものを残し、余計な迷いや手間を減らすことです。</strong>「数を減らせたか」だけでは、よくなったかどうかは決まりません。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:table {"hasFixedLayout":false} -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">どちらを見る？</th><th scope="col">分かりやすさの例</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>作る人が読むプログラム</td><td>どの指示が何をしていて、どこを直せばよいか分かる。</td></tr><tr><td>使う人が触るアプリ</td><td>何を入力し、どこを押せば用事が済むか分かる。</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>使う人の操作が少なくても、裏側の処理まで少ないとは限りません。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"ja: プログラムは「何をする手順か」が分かるようにします"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">プログラムは「何をする手順か」が分かるようにします</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":96,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/02-clear-instructions.png" alt="子どもとロボットが、本・カレンダー・お知らせの絵カードを順番に並べるイラスト" class="wp-image-96"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>借りた本を返す日が来たら、知らせるロボットを考えます。「本のことを、いい感じにお願い」だけでは、何をするのか分かりません。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>そこで、<strong>まだ返していない本のうち、返す日が今日か、それより前の本について知らせる</strong>と決めます。本の名前・返す日・返したかどうかを記録しておきます。正しい日付が保存されている前提で、1冊ずつ次を確かめます。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>もう返した本なら、その本については知らせずに終わる。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>まだ返していない本なら、返す日と今日の日付を比べる。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>返す日が今日か、それより前なら「返す日を確認してね」と知らせる。未来の日なら、まだ知らせない。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>「返したか調べる」「日付を比べる」「知らせる」と役割を分けると、手順を追いやすくなります。お知らせの文章だけ変えたいときも、直す場所を見つけやすくなります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>指示を短くしたくて「返したか調べる」を消すと、返した本についても知らせてしまいます。<strong>短さより、必要な約束を守り、あとから読み直せることが大切です。</strong>ここでは、1回確かめたときに知らせるかどうかだけを考えます。知らせる時刻や回数は、別に決める必要があります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>手順を順番に考える練習は、<a href="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/2026/10/27/programming-logical-thinking-kids/" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">プログラミングと論理的思考力の関係性について</a>でも、身近な例を使って説明しています。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"ja: アプリは「用事が済むまでの迷い」を減らします"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">アプリは「用事が済むまでの迷い」を減らします</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":97,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/03-easy-to-use.png" alt="借りた本と返す日を記録する画面を、入力欄の多い画面と比べる子どものイラスト" class="wp-image-97"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>今度は、ロボットの裏側ではなく、使う画面を考えます。目的は「借りた本と返す日を、あとで確認できるようにすること」です。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>記録するたびに、本の名前、返す日、表紙の写真、感想、好きな登場人物まで必ず入力するとしたらどうでしょう。返す日を覚えておきたいだけなのに、読書感想文を始めるような手間がかかります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>本の名前と返す日を保存し、一覧で見られれば、この目的を果たせます。お知らせも使うなら、返した本に「返却済み」の印を付けられるようにします。感想は、書きたい人があとで足せる形にできます。<strong>みんなに全部を求めず、その場で必要なことに絞る</strong>考え方です。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ただし、返す日まで消してしまうと、「いつ返すか」を確かめられません。また、全部を一つの画面に詰め込むと、かえって見づらくなる場合もあります。画面やボタンの数だけでなく、用事を終えられるかを見ます。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>必要な情報は、使う人によって変わります。自分が借りた本だけを管理するなら借りた人の名前は省けても、図書室全体で使うなら「誰に貸したか」も必要です。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>そもそも、紙のメモで忘れず返せているなら、それで十分です。家族と同じ一覧を見たいなど、今の方法で困ることが残ったらアプリを考えます。題材の絞り方は、<a href="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/2026/10/21/personal-problem-to-app/" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">自分の悩み・困り事を自作アプリにする方法</a>につながります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"ja: アプリの安全を守る仕組みや、間違いを取り消す方法は残します"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">アプリの安全を守る仕組みや、間違いを取り消す方法は残します</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":98,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/04-keep-safety.png" alt="使いやすい取っ手のあるロッカーと、内側で安全を守る鍵の仕組みを描いたイラスト" class="wp-image-98"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>ロッカーの扉は、取っ手を動かすだけで開くと使いやすいですね。でも、「部品を減らそう」と鍵まで外したら、大事な物を守れなくなります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>アプリでも、使う人が見ていないところで必要な仕事があります。たとえば、他の人の記録を勝手に見られないようにすることです。画面をすっきりさせたいからといって、こうした守りまでなくしてよいわけではありません。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>本の返却メモなら、次も確かめます。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>本の名前や返す日が空なら、保存する前に入力を求めるか。「2月30日」のような存在しない日付も見つけられるか。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>保存に失敗したら、「保存できた」と思い込ませない表示になるか。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>間違って消したとき、取り消せるか。取り消せないなら、消す前に確かめられるか。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>たとえば、毎回出る確認画面を減らし、削除後に「元に戻す」を用意する方法も考えられます。どちらが合うかは、消す物の大切さや、確実に戻せるかで決めます。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>使う人の操作を分かりやすくするために、作る側の仕事が増えることはあります。</strong>その場合も、裏側を何でも複雑にしてよいわけではありません。何のための仕組みかを説明でき、直す場所を追えるように整理します。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>シンプルにすれば、間違いが絶対になくなるわけでもありません。必要な働きが残っているか、実際に動かして確かめることが必要です。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"ja: シンプルにできたかは、紙の画面と実際の動きで確かめます"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">シンプルにできたかは、紙の画面と実際の動きで確かめます</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":99,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/05-paper-test.png" alt="二人の子どもが、本の返却メモを紙に描き、説明なしで使えるか試すイラスト" class="wp-image-99"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>「どこまで減らせばよいの？」と迷ったら、数を決める前に、使う場面を書いてみます。パソコンを使わず、紙とえんぴつから始められます。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>誰の、どんな用事かを書く。</strong>例は「自分が、借りた本の返す日を確かめたい」です。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>必要な情報と画面を描く。</strong>本の名前、返す日、保存するボタン、保存した本の一覧を用意します。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>使う人に試してもらう。</strong>「本を1冊記録して、返す日を見つけてください」と頼み、どこで止まるかを見ます。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>止まった理由を一つずつ直す。</strong>ボタンの意味が不明なら名前を変えます。必要な情報がないなら足します。</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>紙では、画面の分かりやすさを考えられます。保存やお知らせが本当に動くかは、アプリを作ってから別に確かめます。入力の確認と、保存した本に知らせる判断を分けて、次の例を試します。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:table {"hasFixedLayout":false} -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">確かめる場面</th><th scope="col">入力や本の状態</th><th scope="col">予想する結果</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>保存する前</td><td>本の名前や返す日が空、または日付が存在しない</td><td>入力を直すよう伝え、そのまま保存しない。</td></tr><tr><td>保存した本を確認する</td><td>まだ返していない・返す日は今日</td><td>返す日を確認するよう知らせる。</td></tr><tr><td>保存した本を確認する</td><td>まだ返していない・返す日は昨日</td><td>返す日を確認するよう知らせる。</td></tr><tr><td>保存した本を確認する</td><td>まだ返していない・返す日は明日</td><td>まだ知らせない。</td></tr><tr><td>保存した本を確認する</td><td>もう返した・返す日は今日</td><td>知らせない。</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>試す相手がいなければ、時間を空けて自分で紙の画面をたどってみてください。他の人に使ってもらう代わりにはなりませんが、言葉や情報の抜けに気づく手がかりになります。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>プログラムやアプリのシンプルさは、「目的を果たせるか」「安全を守れるか」「作る人が仕組みを追えるか」で確かめます。</strong>普段使う道具を一つ選び、「迷った場所」と「なくなると困るもの」を書くところから始めてみてください。</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>

<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>I believe programs and apps should be made simple while keeping the features and protections people need.</strong> Removing unnecessary complexity helps makers follow how things work and users find the actions they need.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A “program” is a set of instructions that makes a computer do something. A “product” is something made for people to use. Here, we will use apps on phones and computers as examples.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>This article builds on <a href="https://zenn.dev/nanashinogonbei/articles/should-programs-and-products-be-simple" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">the Zenn article “Should Programs and Products Be Simple When Possible?”</a> Pencil cases and borrowed books help explain simplicity for makers and users. The apps and situations below are fictional examples.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"en: Simplicity in programs and apps is not just about quantity"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Simplicity in programs and apps is not just about quantity</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":95,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/01-pencil-case.png" alt="An organized pencil case with the stationery needed for school beside an overfilled pencil case" class="wp-image-95"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Think about a pencil case. If pencils and an eraser are mixed with little toys and scraps of paper, finding what you need becomes harder.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Taking out unused things and arranging your stationery makes the case easier to use. But if you empty it completely, you cannot write.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Making something simple means keeping what the task needs and removing unnecessary confusion and effort.</strong> Counting how much you removed does not tell you whether you improved it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:table {"hasFixedLayout":false} -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">What are we looking at?</th><th scope="col">What clarity looks like</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>The program a maker reads</td><td>It is clear what each instruction does and where a change belongs.</td></tr><tr><td>The app a person uses</td><td>It is clear what to enter and what to press to finish the task.</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Fewer steps for the user do not necessarily mean less work behind the scenes.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"en: Make it clear what each step in a program does"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Make it clear what each step in a program does</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":96,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/02-clear-instructions.png" alt="A child and a robot arranging picture cards of a book, a calendar, and a reminder in order" class="wp-image-96"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Imagine a robot that reminds you when it is time to return a borrowed book. “Please take care of the books somehow” does not explain what it should do.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Instead, we decide to <strong>send a reminder for books that have not been returned and whose return date is today or earlier.</strong> Record each book's name, return date, and whether it has been returned. Assuming a valid date has been saved, check each book as follows.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>If the book has already been returned, finish checking that book without sending a reminder.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>If it has not been returned, compare its return date with today's date.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>If the return date is today or earlier, say “Please check the return date.” If the date is in the future, do not send a reminder yet.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Separating the jobs of checking whether a book was returned, comparing dates, and sending a reminder makes the steps easier to follow. It also helps us find where to change just the reminder's wording.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If we remove the returned-book check just to shorten the instructions, the robot may remind us about books we already returned. <strong>Keeping the necessary rules and making instructions readable later matters more than making them short.</strong> Here we consider only whether to send a reminder during one check. The time and frequency of reminders need separate decisions.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For more practice planning steps, <a href="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/2026/10/27/programming-logical-thinking-kids/" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">The Connection Between Programming and Logical Thinking</a> uses everyday examples too.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"en: Help people finish their task with less confusion"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Help people finish their task with less confusion</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":97,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/03-easy-to-use.png" alt="A child comparing a screen for recording a borrowed book and its return date with a screen full of input boxes" class="wp-image-97"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now think about the screen people use, rather than the robot's inner workings. The goal is to record a borrowed book and its return date so you can check them later.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What if every entry required the book's name, return date, a cover photo, your thoughts, and your favorite character? You only wanted to remember the date, but entering it would feel like starting a book report.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Saving a book's name and return date and showing them in a list meets this goal. If reminders are included, let people mark books as returned. People who want to add their thoughts can do so later. <strong>Ask for what the task needs now, instead of making everybody provide everything.</strong></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But removing the return date would stop people checking when to return the book. Putting everything on one screen can also make it harder to read. Look at whether people can finish the task, not just how many screens or buttons remain.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The information needed depends on who uses the app. A list of books you have borrowed may not need a borrower's name, but a school library needs to know who borrowed each book.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If a paper note already helps you return books on time, that is enough. Consider an app when a problem remains, such as needing the same list to be available to your family. For choosing a small problem to work on, see <a href="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/2026/10/21/personal-problem-to-app/" style="color:#0000ff;text-decoration:underline;">How to Turn a Personal Problem into a Small App</a>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"en: Keep app protections and ways to undo mistakes"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Keep app protections and ways to undo mistakes</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":98,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/04-keep-safety.png" alt="A locker with an easy-to-use handle and an inner lock mechanism that protects what is inside" class="wp-image-98"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A locker is easy to use when you can open the door by moving its handle. But removing its lock to reduce the number of parts would leave your belongings unprotected.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Apps also need to do work that users cannot see, such as stopping someone from viewing another person's private records. Making a screen less crowded does not justify removing those protections.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For our book-return list, we should also check these points.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Does the app ask for a missing book name or return date before saving? Does it also catch dates that do not exist, such as February 30?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>If saving fails, does the screen avoid making the user think it succeeded?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Can an accidental deletion be undone? If it cannot, can the user check before deleting?</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For example, we could reduce repeated confirmation screens and offer an “Undo” option after deletion. The right choice depends on how important the deleted item is and whether it can reliably be restored.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Making an app easier to use can mean more work for its maker.</strong> That does not mean anything goes behind the scenes. Each part should have an explainable purpose, and makers should be able to trace where a change belongs.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Simplicity does not guarantee that there will be no mistakes. We still need to run the app and check that its required features work.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph --></div>
<!-- /wp:group -->

<!-- wp:group {"metadata":{"name":"en: Check simplicity with paper screens and the working app"},"className":"simple-product-panel","layout":{"type":"default"}} -->
<div class="wp-block-group simple-product-panel"><!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Check simplicity with paper screens and the working app</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":99,"sizeSlug":"full","linkDestination":"none"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img src="https://www.nanasinogonbei.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/05-paper-test.png" alt="Two children testing paper screens for a book-return list to see whether they make sense without guidance" class="wp-image-99"/></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you are unsure how much to remove, write down the situation before choosing a number of features. You can start with paper and a pencil, without a computer.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Write who needs to do what.</strong> For example: “I want to check when I must return a borrowed book.”</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Draw the information and screens needed.</strong> Include the book's name, return date, a save button, and a list of saved books.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Ask a user to try it.</strong> Say “Please record one book and find its return date,” then watch where they get stuck.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->

<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Address one cause at a time.</strong> Rename a button if its meaning is unclear. Add information if something needed is missing.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Paper helps you think about whether screens make sense. Check whether saving and reminders actually work once the app exists. Test input checks separately from reminder decisions for saved books, using these examples.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:table {"hasFixedLayout":false} -->
<figure class="wp-block-table"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Stage being checked</th><th scope="col">Input or book's state</th><th scope="col">Expected result</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Before saving</td><td>The book's name or return date is missing, or the date does not exist</td><td>Ask for corrected input and do not save it as is.</td></tr><tr><td>Checking a saved book</td><td>Not returned; return date is today</td><td>Ask the user to check the return date.</td></tr><tr><td>Checking a saved book</td><td>Not returned; return date was yesterday</td><td>Ask the user to check the return date.</td></tr><tr><td>Checking a saved book</td><td>Not returned; return date is tomorrow</td><td>Do not send a reminder yet.</td></tr><tr><td>Checking a saved book</td><td>Already returned; return date is today</td><td>Do not send a reminder.</td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!-- /wp:table -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If nobody is available to try it, take a break and then follow the paper screens yourself. This does not replace watching another person use them, but it may help you notice unclear words or missing information.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Judge simplicity in programs and apps by asking: “Can people finish the task?”, “Are they protected?”, and “Can makers follow how it works?”</strong> Start with one tool you use regularly. Write down where you get confused and what you could not do without.</p>
